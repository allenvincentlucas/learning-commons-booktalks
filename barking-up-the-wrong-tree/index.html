Are we barking up the wrong tree?

01 The inquiry: what actually drives success?

As IB students, teachers, and lifelong learners, we spend a massive amount of time talking about success. We talk about optimal study habits, time management, leadership, grit, and building a well-rounded portfolio.

But have you ever paused to ask yourself: where did these rules come from, and are they actually true?

In his Wall Street Journal bestseller, Barking Up the Wrong Tree: The Surprising Science Behind Why Everything You Know About Success Is (Mostly) Wrong, author Eric Barker takes a scientific lens to the maxims we take for granted. Drawing on evidence-based research, sociology, historical anecdotes, and a healthy dose of humor, Barker puts standard self-help clichés on trial.

If you've ever felt like you're playing strictly by the rules but not getting the results you expected, this book is designed to make you re-evaluate the whole playing field.

02 Key themes for the curious mind

No spoilers — just the dilemmas Barker puts on trial.

Achievement
Filtered vs. unfiltered trajectories

Why do high school valedictorians rarely go on to change the world in earth-shattering ways, while famous dropouts and eccentrics often disrupt whole industries? What does our education system actually measure?

Cooperation
The "jerk" conundrum

Does being nice mean finishing last, or do cooperative strategies win in the long run? Barker evaluates tactics ranging from corporate boardrooms to surprising historical subcultures.

Persistence
The strategic role of quitting

We are constantly told that "quitters never win." But is blind grit always smart, or is knowing when to pivot a core component of mastery?

Personality
Introverts, extroverts, and networks

Extroverts might excel in immediate social settings, but introverts often cultivate extraordinary expertise. How do different personality structures navigate connection without feeling transactional?

Rather than handing down simple, one-size-fits-all answers, Barker demonstrates that context is everything. A trait that is a liability in one environment might be a superpower in another.

05 Reflect and discuss

A few prompts for a CAS reflection, an advisory circle, or a TOK exhibition proposal.

  • Think of a "rule" for success you were taught early on. Has Barker's research changed how much you trust it?
  • Barker argues that context determines whether a trait is a strength or a weakness. Name a trait of yours and describe two contexts where it would land differently.
  • If you had to defend one piece of conventional success advice against Barker's evidence, which would you choose, and why?

07 About the author

EB

Eric Barker

Eric Barker writes the blog Barking Up the Wrong Tree, which distills behavioral science into practical advice on living and working well. A former Hollywood screenwriter with a degree from the University of Pennsylvania and graduate degrees from Boston College and UCLA, he has spoken at MIT, Yale, and Google. This is his first book.
