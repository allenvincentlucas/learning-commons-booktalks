01 The inquiry: what if home was never really about the house?

To Queenie, home is Peachy — the little peach-walled house with flaky paint she's lived in her whole life, ever since her dad died. So when her mum tells her Peachy has been sold and they have to leave, right at the end of a hard school term, it feels like losing everything all over again. What follows is a single summer where Queenie and her mum move seven times: a retirement village, a leaky old camper van, a shared house, and more, never quite landing anywhere that feels permanent.

Author Zanni Louise didn't invent this situation. In 2020, her own family had to leave their rental and move seven times over one summer as the pandemic-era housing market made stable rentals almost impossible to find. She wrote Queenie in Seven Moves out of that experience, which is part of why the book never treats housing insecurity as a plot twist to feel sorry for — it treats it as something ordinary and real that a kid has to find a way through.

Along the way, Queenie has to patch things up with a former best friend she now sees as a rival, work up the nerve to perform in front of people, and slowly figure out that the thing she's been missing isn't really the house at all. It's feeling safe enough, and known enough, wherever she happens to be.

For a younger reader who has ever had to start over somewhere new — a new school, a new house, a new town — Queenie's summer is a gentle but honest way into a question worth sitting with: what actually makes somewhere feel like home?

02 Key themes for the curious mind

No spoilers — just the tensions the story sits inside.

Belonging
What actually makes a place home?

Queenie starts the summer believing home is a specific house. By the end, she's tested that idea against seven different temporary places. What does the story suggest actually makes somewhere feel like home, if it isn't the walls themselves?

Resilience
Getting comfortable with being uncomfortable

Every move forces Queenie into a new, unfamiliar situation before she's ready for it. What does she gain from having to adjust again and again, that she wouldn't have gained by staying in one place the whole time?

Friendship
When an old friend becomes a rival

Queenie and Sparrow used to be close, and now Queenie sees her as competition. How much of that rivalry is really about talent, and how much is about hurt feelings that were never actually resolved?

Honesty
What grown-ups don't tell kids

Queenie's mum keeps things from her, hoping to protect her from worry. Whose comfort is that secrecy really protecting — and does keeping a hard truth from someone actually make things easier for them in the end?

These tensions build gently across one long, unsettled summer — the book takes its time letting Queenie work out what home really means to her, one temporary address at a time.

04 Theory of Knowledge connection

TOK lensInquiry connection
Scope and contextThis story is fiction, but it's based on the author's own real experience of losing stable housing. How does turning a true, difficult experience into a story change how a reader understands it, compared to reading a news report about the same issue?
Methods and toolsQueenie tells her own story, in her own voice, as a kid living through these moves firsthand. How is what we learn from a kid's-eye view of a big problem different from what adults or experts usually tell us about it?
PerspectivesQueenie's mum hides some hard truths from her, hoping to protect her. Whose comfort is that protection really serving — and is keeping a difficult truth from someone always the same thing as protecting them?

05 Reflect and discuss

A few prompts for a CAS reflection, an advisory circle, or a class discussion.

  • Queenie's mum keeps the truth about their housing situation from her for a while, hoping to protect her. Think of a time an adult in your life kept something hard from you, or you did the same for someone younger. Did it actually help, or did it make things harder later?
  • Every time Queenie moves, she has to start over in some way — a new space, a new routine, sometimes new people. What has helped you, or could help someone you know, feel more at home somewhere unfamiliar?
  • Queenie's rivalry with her old friend Sparrow is really about hurt feelings, not about who's more talented. Think of a friendship that changed because of jealousy or a misunderstanding. What would it take to fix it?

07 About the author

ZL

Zanni Louise

Zanni Louise is an Australian author of over forty books for children and teens, from picture books to young adult novels. Born and raised in country New South Wales, she now splits her time between the Byron Bay hinterland and the Netherlands. She holds a Bachelor of Arts in Art History and Creative Writing, along with a Graduate Diploma of Psychology and a Certificate in Counselling. Queenie in Seven Moves, her first novel for older readers, was inspired directly by her own family's experience in 2020, when a rental sale forced them to move seven times over a single summer. The novel now features on the NSW school curriculum, and Louise regularly visits schools and festivals to run writing workshops for young readers.
