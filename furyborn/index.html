A BookTalk on Furyborn by Claire Legrand

01 The inquiry: what if your future was decided before you were born?

An ancient prophecy says two queens will rise: one of light, who will save the world, and one of blood, who will destroy it. When Rielle Dardenne accidentally exposes her ability to wield every kind of elemental magic — a power that should belong only to one of those prophesied queens — she isn't celebrated. She's put on trial, forced to endure seven brutal tests to prove she's the queen of salvation and not the one of ruin, with her own execution as the alternative if she fails.

A thousand years later, Eliana Ferracora has never wielded magic like Rielle's. She's a bounty hunter for the empire that conquered her kingdom, and the legend of Queen Rielle is just a bedtime story to her — until her mother disappears, and Eliana is pulled into a war that reaches back to the very prophecy she never believed in.

Told in alternating chapters across two timelines a thousand years apart, the book keeps returning to the same uncomfortable question: what does it do to a person to be handed a destiny — savior or destroyer — before anyone has bothered to ask who they actually are? Rielle's trials aren't really about her magic. They're about whether the people around her can imagine her as anything other than one of two extremes.

For a student who's ever felt boxed in by what people expected of them before they'd had the chance to prove otherwise, Rielle and Eliana's parallel stories are worth sitting with — not for a tidy answer about fate versus free will, but for how uncomfortable the book makes that question feel.

02 Key themes for the curious mind

No spoilers — just the tensions the story sits inside.

Destiny
Prophecy as trap, not gift

Rielle's power should be a triumph. Instead, it puts her on trial for her life. What does it cost a person when the same trait that makes them extraordinary is also the trait everyone fears most about them?

Duality
Two women, one thousand years apart

Rielle and Eliana never meet, yet their stories mirror and eventually intersect across a millennium. What does telling their stories in parallel let the book reveal about how the past continues to shape the present, long after the people who lived it are gone?

Power
The cost of extraordinary ability

Both Rielle and Eliana are more powerful than almost anyone around them — and both pay for it, in scrutiny, in isolation, in violence expected of them that they didn't choose. Is being exceptional ever actually a privilege in this world, or only ever a liability?

Morality
Queen of light or queen of blood?

The prophecy gives the world exactly two boxes to put Rielle in, with nothing in between. Is any person — in fiction or otherwise — actually sortable into only one of two categories, or does that binary say more about the people doing the sorting than about her?

These tensions build slowly across both timelines — the book takes its time letting Rielle's trials and Eliana's manhunt complicate each other before their true connection comes into focus.

04 Theory of Knowledge connection

TOK lensInquiry connection
Scope and contextFantasy invents its own prophecies and magic systems. Can an invented framework like this still meaningfully explore real questions about determinism and free will, or does the fantasy distance let the story avoid the harder version of the question?
Methods and toolsWhat does alternating between two heroines a thousand years apart let the author reveal about how the past shapes the present that a single, linear timeline could not?
PerspectivesRielle's entire identity is shaped by how others interpret an ancient prophecy about her, rather than by anything she has personally chosen. How much of any person's identity — in fiction or in life — is actually built by other people's interpretations rather than their own choices?

05 Reflect and discuss

A few prompts for a CAS reflection, an advisory circle, or a TOK exhibition proposal.

  • Rielle is put on trial not for anything she's done, but for a power she was born with and can't fully control. Where in real history or current events do people face judgment based on identity or potential rather than action — and what does the book suggest about the fairness of that?
  • The prophecy frames Rielle's future as strictly binary: savior or destroyer, with no space in between. Think of a real belief system, institution, or narrative that flattens people into similarly binary roles. What gets lost when a person's future is treated as already decided?
  • The book splits its story across two heroines living a thousand years apart. What did structuring the story this way let you notice about how the past shapes the present that a single, linear story might have hidden?

07 About the author

CL

Claire Legrand

Claire Legrand is a New York Times bestselling author from Irving, Texas, now living in New Jersey. She originally set out to be a musician before her stories pulled her toward writing instead, earning a Bachelor's in English Literature and a Master's in Library Science from the University of North Texas and briefly working as a librarian. Legrand first conceived of Furyborn and the wider Empirium Trilogy at eighteen, the same year she graduated high school, and spent over a decade developing it alongside her debut novel, The Cavendish Home for Boys and Girls (2012). She's also known for the YA horror novel Sawkill Girls, a Bram Stoker Award finalist, and the adult fantasy Middlemist Trilogy.
